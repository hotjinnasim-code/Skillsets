# General style diagnosis — frozen H, read only

This is a qualitative design diagnosis, not an independent critic score or a claim that any complete case passed. No runtime files, frozen plans, prompts, images, selections or critic inputs were changed. No images were generated. The human explicitly chose to lower both the clay/fat-letter/loud-complementary/large-blob combination and recurring large-object/large-title composition in future default directions. H remains frozen.

## Main finding

H contains the right intentions, but often accepts a convincing verbal explanation of a relationship before checking whether the design decision changes the visible editorial structure. “The title names the recovery,” “counterweight,” “shared origin,” “participates in descent” can all resolve into an ordinary bold title beside a large illustration. The instructions already say to avoid repeated layouts; adding that sentence again is unlikely to help. The next revision needs a short, operational comparison before generation and a check against the resulting structure afterward.

The compulsory substantial non-glyph concept appears to be interpreted as a compulsory oversized autonomous object in several directions. Substantial should mean that the non-glyph element carries the idea and remains perceptible, not that it must dominate the area or become a single isolated hero object. Preserve the human’s prohibition on type/whitespace-only output; do not solve this by retreating to empty typographic posters.

## Cross-case observations and useful controls

- D1 M2, M3 and M5 use substantially different subjects/processes, yet all select a heavy, three-word stacked black title. Their coordinator descriptions give the title different metaphorical roles, but the actual typographic grouping has little variation. M3’s gear subject is directly legible; M5’s viscosity contrast is directly legible. Neither positive feature establishes that the title treatment itself is distinctive or sophisticated.
- D1 M1 is a useful control: paused versus passing bodies, selective blur and a common ground convey the title’s meaning without a rendered studio object. Its restrained horizontal title is meaningfully different from the other viewed title stacks. Keep the capacity for such direct, observational scenes. This is not a complete craft or copy pass: I did not conduct all-size transcription or anatomy auditing.
- B02’s three selected posters genuinely differ in information location: side column, central opening, and programme above a central shared scene. The programme/participation groups are useful editorial decisions to retain. However, navy heavy sans plus green foliage and pleasing architecture converge across all three. Architecture and trees can read as a generic urban-development illustration even when the planning prose describes observing, uncovering and assembling data. Test whether the visual relation to data is discernible without the explanation; do not require invented charts, numbers or pseudo factual maps to make it “data-like.”
- B04 M1, M3 and M5 are explicitly constrained by the simulated client to clay 3D, raised thick readable Hangul, strong complements and a large deforming non-glyph clay body. Those features are request fidelity in H and must not be retrospectively scored as violations of a new default preference. The selected M3 visibly connects flattening to swelling in one body, which is a useful relationship control. Yet the large body, large rounded letters, uniform rendered texture and saturated complementary backdrop still produce the package the human rejected. Within an explicit genre, craft and editorial decisions must improve; under an open brief, that combination should lose default priority.

## Concise reusable changes to consider for candidate I

1. **Select an editorial operation before naming a medium.** Start with what the reader must notice or do, and what relation in the content makes that possible. Decide how the actual words, image boundary and information groups participate. Then choose the medium that can execute it. A medium or subject substitution alone is insufficient differentiation.
2. **Make the pre-generation comparison concrete.** In the joint direction plan, record one visible typographic/space decision per direction and its nearest neighbour: actual word grouping or reading plane, title’s relative prominence, and the boundary/alignment that links it to the meaningful visual. Compare the planned page structures with style names and material descriptions removed. If they reduce to the same large title plus autonomous object plus remaining information, replace a redundant direction before calling the tool. Do not force every axis to differ or impose a layout quota.
3. **Tie the type decision to actual copy.** Before allocating scene area, consider the longest required line, language pairs and meaningful word groups. A “balanced, modern, bold sans” sentence is not a typography decision. Describe the chosen width/weight/line grouping and why the surrounding geometry supports it. Preserve normal readable proportions; do not manufacture uniqueness through arbitrary stretching, scattering or letter damage.
4. **Lower formula combinations under open briefs.** Do not lead with oversized toy/clay blobs, inflated rounded headings and loud complementary backdrops; also lower the broader large-object/large-title composition when it is justified only by generic visual impact. The rule is a preference among candidate directions, not a ban on 3D, bright colour, direct objects, photography, or familiar type. Explicit client requests retain priority.
5. **Use constructive alternatives at the relationship level.** Let a visible action, an observed situation, a meaningful boundary, a connected system or information organisation generate the page. Consider a subject distributed through the frame rather than always isolated, or an image boundary that actively orders the copy. These are possible operations, not a mandatory replacement style menu; choose only what the brief supports. Keep a substantial non-glyph idea in every output.
6. **Inspect default fingerprints as combinations.** Look for unnecessary all-over microtexture; toy/studio volume without a content reason; stock “pleasant city” foliage; repeated heavy title stacks; generic symmetrical framing; rounded information cards added independently of the image logic. A combination that survives changing only the event name is a warning. None of these isolated features proves AI authorship, and none should override explicit client constraints.
7. **Shorten the native prompt while retaining full maker context.** The viewed native inputs prepend the complete request, copy JSON, common conditions, coordinator constraints and then repeat the chosen concept and detailed design. Keep the maker’s full request and verification context, but send a focused native specification containing the exact visible copy once, necessary constraints, the chosen relation, and concrete typography/space decisions. Repairs should state the change and protected relationships. Prompt length is an observed instrumentation issue, not a proven cause of the images’ style.
8. **Validate the proposed mechanism on the actual image.** A title described as a counterweight or origin must visibly perform that role; proximity alone is not enough. Compare the complete set after generation, preserving direct scenes and functional information structures that work. A correction should target a failed editorial relation before adding texture or polishing surfaces. Do not declare high-end quality because the resulting image is legible or the prompt uses that adjective.
