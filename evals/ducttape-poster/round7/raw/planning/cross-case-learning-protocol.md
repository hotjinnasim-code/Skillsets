# Reusable skill improvement, candidate H to a later revision

The requested outcome is a better runtime skill across future user requests. The twenty H deliverables test the current frozen skill; repaired pictures alone do not establish that improvement.

Close all twenty selections before independent critics start. Preserve initial, selected, unused repairs, failed attempts, raw reviews, low scores and ambiguity. Do not change H or inject new guidance into a role after its run starts.

After complete independent reviews, identify a decision failure from visible evidence and trace it to a reusable skill stage: interpreting constraints, choosing the visual proposition, composing information and image, specifying typography, resolving material or mechanics, or selecting a correction. Distinguish a recurring cause from a symptom and from reviewer preference. Use failures across different requests and successful examples as controls. A single mandatory violation can justify a general safeguard, but not a bespoke template for that test picture.

For each proposed instruction change, record the supporting role/profile/evidence location, why the current instruction did not prevent the failure, a success it must preserve, and the behavior the new instruction changes before generation or during verification. Do not ship test titles, preferred palettes, sample layouts, genre bans or a memorized set of five styles as the solution. Primary design references support principles; they do not override explicit client styles or copy.

Only after this diagnosis, materially revise the reusable runtime instructions and freeze a new candidate. The H images used to decide that revision are development evidence for the new candidate. They cannot certify its held-out performance. Test the new candidate on literal new client requests not used in its instruction revision, with input and budget pins fixed before generation. Failed new tests may inform a subsequent materially different revision, while their original outcomes remain intact.

Report finite observed results and limitations. Do not equate an individual repair, a mean score, a best-of-set selection or a small passing sample with high-end quality for every future prompt.
