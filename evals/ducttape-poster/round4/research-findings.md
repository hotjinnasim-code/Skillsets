## Three runtime/evaluation changes supported by this research

1. **Check semantic grouping and actual reading conditions.** Keep all supplied facts and their event/role/date/place/access/response relationships. Same-level elements share a deliberate treatment; different-level information gets a visible difference. Inspect overview and required text at useful reading size. Do not substitute decorative microtype, missing copy or visual busyness for information design.
2. **Choose product framing by function and verify structure.** Inspect connected members, joint logic, thickness, fold/pack state, supports, feet, occlusion and contact shadows. A pretty rendering cannot compensate for impossible hinges, disconnected rails or a portability claim with no legible packed state. If the brief permits multiple states, each should demonstrate something necessary.
3. **Require an observable visual argument while respecting the requested genre.** Ask what the type/image/motif/composition physically does that fits the subject. Contour, cropping, motion, construction or grouping can supply the relation. Familiar civic/festival/product grammar and ordinary sans/serif type are not automatic failures. Evaluate absent decisions, interchangeable imagery, broken groups, arbitrary decoration and incoherent construction instead. Do not demand a new font, texture or handmade gesture on every request.

These are application inferences from selected original works, not statistical style rankings. They support review of actual outputs; they cannot guarantee universal future quality or prove human authorship. The sources' wall/phone/street documentation does not change the flat-poster deliverable requirement.
