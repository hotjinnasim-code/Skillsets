Viewed file: `research/primary-reference-cache/feixen-kulturbuero-poster.png`.

| Dimension | My whole-image observation |
|---|---|
| Image/style and relation | Enormous black letter masses intersect broad flowing red, violet and white fields; the image is a continuous graphic construction rather than an illustrative central object. Grid patches alter the surface rhythm. |
| Viewpoint/crop | The whole clipped sheet is visible against grey. Inside its printed rectangle, letters and coloured fields run beyond the edges; their incompleteness is intentional at the large scale. |
| Layout/space | Successive dense horizontal word zones are crossed by a shared diagonal movement. Narrow small-type blocks occupy the top edge. The crossing fields bind the zones instead of enclosing each in its own card. |
| Colour | Near-black remains strong over white, red, violet and gridded grey. Red is strongest at upper left; violet spreads through the middle and lower right. White is an active continuous shape. |
| Typography | Very heavy compact sans becomes the dominant physical mass. Small upper blocks are also firm sans but use entirely different scale and density. The cropped large letters cannot provide the same reading function as the intact small blocks. |
| Information hierarchy | The giant words provide the distant impact; facts stay grouped in top-edge islands. This is not a universally applicable reading order for a long Korean participation brief. |
| Material/making | Two clips and the grey surround show the sheet as a photographed object. Thin grid lines contrast with thick type. Neither paper stock nor a physical printing technique is established. |
| Emotion/audience | I read the large contrast and crossing motion as assertive, experimental public culture. Its legibility tradeoff would need substantial adaptation for first-time local attendees. |

Do not copy its ribbons, alphabet, palette or grids. Its graphic motion alone would not identify rubber printing or shared reading.
