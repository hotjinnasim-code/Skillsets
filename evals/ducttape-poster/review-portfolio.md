Across all five E images, the differentiation is substantive and not a palette swap: E1 uses a photographic curl, E2 makes a pressure/ink-like typographic band, E3 uses a halftone field, E4 uses slit-and-fold repetition, and E5 illustrates a print roller. Composition changes as well: centered horizontal type, image-crossing type, a bottom label, and type angled with a sheet are all represented. Nonetheless, E1 and E4 share the same paper-sculpture/lighting family and both rely on conventional black sans-serif labels. E3 and E5 share related calligraphic lettering. I would describe this as five distinguishable treatments with family overlap, rather than confidently certify five fully separate expression systems. E2, E3, and E5 carry the printing part of the brief more directly than E1 and E4.

**E3 is the weakest visually resolved image in the original five**, chiefly because its prominent title has the least controlled contrast. E4 has the clearest delivery/presentation ambiguity, and E1/E4 have the most generalized subject connection. These are visible limitations, not objections to halftones, paper photography, minimalism, or rough texture as categories.

**Preservation is successful at the level of copy and macro layout, but incomplete under a strict “change only the date; retain the image” reading.** Native side-by-side inspection shows changed fine surface texture well outside the date, including the open central-left wall, upper wall, and around the title. This is not merely a claim about file encoding: comparing decoded RGB pixels while excluding a conservative date rectangle (0-based x=45–557, y=1245–1337) finds changes at 99.548% of the remaining pixel positions, with mean absolute per-channel difference 9.01/255. That broad difference does **not** mean the layout or subject changed by 99.548%; even tiny rendering differences count. It does establish that untouched pixels were not retained. The visible texture differences also make the limitation perceptual, despite the strong resemblance at overview size. I would accept the date/copy/layout checks and flag image/texture preservation as partial.
