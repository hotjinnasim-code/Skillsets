# Blind design review: anonymous initial packet

This review used only `blind-initial/cases.json`, `rubric.md`, and the 16 anonymous PNGs in each of `native`, `usage`, and `thumb`. Every native original was individually viewed, followed by its 720 px wide usage image and 320 px long-edge thumbnail. No mappings, prompts, maker notes, skills, or other reviews were read. The scores are separate judgments, with no total or average.

The result is mixed. T is my modest preference for the garden alternatives, with moderate confidence, because its title roles and composition vary more and T4 is the strongest garden treatment. S has strong alternatives in S1 and S3. Neither set is consistently superior across its five images. S5 is the weakest image because the only required message becomes a tiny, subdued column at thumbnail scale. There are credible high-end candidates here, but this packet does not support an all-case high-end pass or a universal success claim.

The scale anchors used are: 1 = unresolved, 3 = functioning, 5 = very well resolved; 2 and 4 require visible intermediate evidence. A 3 does not imply a user-condition failure. Conversely, correct copy does not establish a 4–5 design result.

## Weakest, strongest, and supported next action

The clearest practical weakness is **S5 in the garden-title-only case**: the title is the sole required information, yet the thumbnail primarily reads as a floral print. Its detailed material is a strength, but it cannot substitute for message hierarchy. The highest-priority fix is to keep the blue botanical banks and quiet mood while making the central title larger and brighter. Three horizontal word rows (`밤에` / `피는` / `정원`) within the corridor would also remove the word-boundary-wrap interpretation problem. Recheck the 320 px image after that change; the goal is immediate phrase retrieval without flattening the border into decoration.

**T4 is the strongest garden image** for actual lettering/illustration integration. **P1 is the strongest feature-demonstration image**, with the expanded/collapsed object states supporting the exact product proposition. **C1 is the strongest action hierarchy** because the deadline and URL remain conspicuous in the thumbnail. **N1 is the stronger explicit-effects direction**, since the title, track, and worn skate belong to one directional scene.

For the other weaker directions, the most supported changes are modest and preserve the requested styles: P2 needs a more specific folding-related visual idea if it is to move beyond a functioning studio ad; N2 needs more distinct roles for the main chrome title, equipment, and supporting copy while keeping strong neon; T2 needs better word grouping and a more specific relationship between title and garden; T5 needs a more convincing relationship between its decorative strip and dominant word blocks. None needs wholesale removal of its genre or a universal minimal-paper treatment.

The rubric's four critical axes—content/form, typography, finish, and slop avoidance—have visible 4–5 evidence in S1, S3, T1, T3, T4, C1, P1, and N1. The garden sets also reach diversity 4.
