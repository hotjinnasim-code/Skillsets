**Portrait: R2 over R7, narrowly.** Its coordinated roles make the communal invitation clearer at 320. R7 is stronger at explaining the exact pickup gesture: its bottle, gripper, and bag are larger. Selecting R2 sacrifices that object-level immediacy; copy, hierarchy, and measured ratio are otherwise effectively tied.

Regression: T3 removes T8's visible two-person participation and replaces the spacious two-column grouping with a denser sentence stack. T8 is still functional; its smaller, flatter practical hierarchy is the clearest weakness among these four.

**Selected-set diversity: 3.** R2 uses a full-width cooperative photo above a separate information panel; T3 integrates an enlarged diagonal pickup scene beside the information. Those are substantive composition and image-role differences, not just palette changes. However, both assign the same dominant role to a huge heavy headline, a left-aligned sequence of practical sentences, and a solid bottom application band. A genre change alone does not warrant 4–5.

**Strongest candidate: T3, narrowly**, for integrating the pickup scene beside complete usable copy and a prominent application strip. R2 is stronger on visible togetherness and larger portrait body copy; no candidate dominates every dimension. **Weakest: T8**, due to the small, similarly weighted practical columns; this is not a penalty for illustration. **Weakest selected core dimension: AI-slop prevention, 3 in both R2 and T3.** The selected set therefore does not meet the rubric's complete high-end signal: that core dimension and diversity remain below 4, despite copy and ratio passing.
