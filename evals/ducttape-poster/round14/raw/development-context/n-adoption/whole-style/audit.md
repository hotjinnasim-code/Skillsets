# Independent whole-style development audit of revised N

Revised N responds to the human's rejection at the level of image, space and information relationships, rather than prescribing a replacement font. Its written rules cover the obvious escapes visible in the selected posters and rejected Canva examples. The remaining concern is operational: a substantial, relevant picture can be treated as evidence of a changed whole style even when the headline/information panel still organizes the poster. I recommend one small addition to the existing visual QA record, not a new style list or a broader blacklist.

## What the selected posters actually show

**M1, initial.** A teal left field carries very large date/time, place and participation blocks. A yellow right field carries the heavy title and subtitle, a bounded photograph, then two reassuring lines. The photograph is substantive: adults look at framed architectural images while a guide speaks in a warm sunlit gallery. This coherently joins the exhibition and talk and suggests approachable adult participation. Nevertheless, its straight rectangular crop remains an insert between text regions. At usage720 the practical groups are easy to locate; at thumbnail320 the two flat fields and dense bold typography still organize the poster. This is a whole relationship problem, not an absence of relevant imagery.

**M2, initial.** The room fills the frame. A tall bright window, framed architectural photographs, chairs and diagonal floor light establish depth; darker walls provide copy space. The white left title is large and heavy, but supporting text is smaller/lighter, and the right-side information uses the room's darker area. The photograph's quiet material and light relationships survive at thumbnail320. This is a meaningful overall difference from the mounted-photo text panels, although it shares warm window/interior imagery with M1 and M3. An empty room and chairs suggest a setting for observation/listening rather than visibly depicting the talk itself. The strongest image-led counterexample in this set would be damaged by a blanket ban on gothic titles or quiet photographic space. Nothing here establishes that the human individually accepted M2.

**M3, initial.** A pale blue page mounts a large room/window photograph and a smaller sill-detail crop. The heavy orange title anchors the lower left, while very large date/time and dark information groups occupy the right. The large photograph is not a tiny decoration; the second crop does create an overview/detail relationship. Yet the cropped images, title and schedule remain separate editorial blocks on a passive field. At thumbnail320 the title/date and two-column structure are conspicuous. Its colour and crop arrangement differ from M1, but the realistic window/light expression remains close to M2. The space-talk connection is conveyed mainly by supplied copy, not a second visible activity. Swapping this title to serif would leave the principal relationship intact.

**M4, repair-1.** A central isometric cutaway shows seated adults listening to a speaker beside framed architecture photographs and a lit window. Drawn surfaces, muted clothing and warm structural materials produce a personable, instructional mood, rather than another realistic photograph. This is substantive visual construction and a directly legible shared event. Bold title/date and ruled information sections flank it on ivory. At usage720 the event and practical groups remain identifiable; at thumbnail320 the cutaway survives but the three separate image/text zones also remain. It is inaccurate to call the illustration tiny or irrelevant. It is also unsafe to declare the whole-style rejection solved merely because the illustration is large. This is the useful borderline case for assessing whether the scene shapes the composition or occupies a slot between pre-existing information panels.

**M5, repair-1.** A near-ivory upper panel holds large dark title, subtitle, date/time and practical information in two columns. A separate lower band repeats perspective windows and bright light planes against navy. The hard-edged geometry and strong contrast create a materially different graphic impression from the photographs and cutaway. The strip gives architecture/light a visible form, while the exhibition/talk relation relies mostly on text. At both usage720 and thumbnail320, the upper announcement panel and lower graphic band stay separate. The strip is conspicuous, not merely a tiny icon; its size and saturated colour still do not reorganize the title/information template.

Across the five, there are actual differences in photographic scene, overview/detail editing, cutaway illustration, geometric construction, crop and mood. They are not identical images. The recurring heavy title/date/information treatment nevertheless compresses several directions into related announcement-panel systems. M2 changes the image/space hierarchy most clearly; M4 changes the expression medium and event depiction, while retaining panel-like flanks. Calling all five “flat background only” would miss this evidence, but counting media labels or palette changes as five fully different whole systems would miss the human's concern.

## What the five references do—and do not—support

The three studio works are **three projects from Version Industries, one studio**, as attributed in the supplied note. They are not three independent studios, five approved directions, or genre-matched answers to this event.

**Anne at 13,000 Feet:** Unequal overlapping photo crops follow a helmeted woman's changing facial expressions in a skydiving sequence. Their stepped displacement organizes extensive white space. The bold sans-serif title and credits are vertical at the far left. The image's emotional progression and editing carry the whole composition despite a plain white field and sans-serif text. This directly shows why blank-area percentage or font family cannot define the rejection. The sequence does not license inventing a timetable or progression for a title-only or event request.

**The Grandfather Puzzle:** Different-scale black and turquoise portrait contours overlap with brushed white, document handwriting and scattered archival-looking family photographs. The large faces organize the page; photographs become fragments within that relationship. A bold top title and compact lower credits have different jobs. Paper and ink expression belong to the collage's memory/puzzle idea rather than simply roughening an information card. Its handwritten material, known people and history are not facts available for a new poster. The JPEG cannot certify actual physical manufacture.

**Protomen / This City Made Us:** Severely cropped foreground boots frame a small receding figure and helmet. Scale, depth, hatching and black shadow organize the orange-red field; the small top label and outlined lower title use its boundaries. A restricted palette and a strong colour field do not make this a passive text template. The scene creates tension and force, which would require a different emotional treatment for a welcoming neighborhood event. Its boots, character, palette, logo and exact crop are not reusable prescriptions.

The two Canva sources are **deliberately rejected whole compositions**. They are useful negative examples, with limited positive information-grouping lessons.

**Brown Photography Workshop:** A dark brown field with soft blurred glow carries an enormous condensed sans-serif heading and a script “Workshop” treatment. A small camera portrait sits in a ruled middle row; date, list columns, fee and a button-like action group form the rest. Glow, one photograph and one script word do not change the governing headline/information-card structure. The useful transferable principle is separating purpose, date and participation/action—not the composition or its added facts.

**Street Portraits:** A broad gray field contains a gold serif title, italic invitation/description and centered instructor/place/date/time groups. A narrow right column holds three black-and-white street photographs. The serif substitution and multiplied photographs leave the broad text panel plus attached image column intact. This is particularly concrete evidence against interpreting the rejection as “replace gothic with serif.”

The source study therefore supports changes in scene organization, editing, scale, crop, boundaries and information roles. It does not support mandatory vintage paper, universal full-bleed photography, giant heroes, a five-medium roster, or compulsory cinematic drama.

## Remaining loophole and minimal reusable recommendation

The remaining loophole is **evidence of enforcement**, not a missing serif/gradient prohibition. M4 contains a large, relevant illustration; M3 contains a substantial photograph. A maker can point to either and say “the image is substantive,” then describe the intended event and record readable first/next anchors. Those observations alone do not establish that the passive field plus title/information-panel relationship has changed. N already says size alone is insufficient ([art-direction.md](/Users/nahyeonbin/Desktop/_Codex/ducttape-poster-n-generalization-review/candidate-n/references/art-direction.md:83)) and demands actual exclusion checks. However, the existing short maker QA record expressly asks for anchors, copy-search and correspondence evidence ([parallel-production.md](/Users/nahyeonbin/Desktop/_Codex/ducttape-poster-n-generalization-review/candidate-n/references/parallel-production.md:25)), without expressly asking the acceptance rationale for this particular whole-style boundary. That makes a borderline judgment easy to assert rather than explain.

Recommend only a short addition to that existing QA note, linked to the canonical definition. Suggested wording:

> 기본 제외를 대조할 때는 축소·사용 보기에서 이미지·장면·편집(명시적 이미지 제외 시 글자·공간)의 어떤 실제 관계가 전체 구성을 조직하는지와 제목·안내 판형에 부가물을 붙인 관계가 남는지를 기존 검수 기록에 짧게 남긴다. 이미지의 존재·면적·재료명만으로 해소를 인정하지 않으며, 필수 정보의 읽히는 묶음이나 단색 여백 자체를 위반 근거로 삼지 않는다.

This consolidates existing criteria into an observable reason for acceptance or unresolved status. It should apply to direct production as well as maker production, using the existing QA mechanism. It adds no numerical image quota, new score, extra evaluator, approval, forced layout, current palette or props. No six-file rewrite or additional composition blacklist is warranted by this evidence. The recommendation is recorded here only; candidate inputs remain untouched.

A harmful interpretation to guard against is “images must always outrank practical information” or “plain copy regions are prohibited.” N's actual wording largely prevents that: different entry anchors are allowed, long copy receives space before it is shrunk, and sans-serif/blank areas themselves remain permissible. Anne and M2 make the distinction visible. Turning the exclusion into a ban on readable grouped information would sacrifice the request while encouraging crowded image overlays.

## Reusable boundaries for other requests

| Request constraint | Appropriate application of N |
| --- | --- |
| Readable long-copy 16:9 | Keep enough usable area for all supplied participation information and assess it at usage size. A readable information group, column or quiet area is allowed; it becomes an exclusion issue through its passive, template-dominating relationship to the rest of the poster. Do not force full-bleed imagery, distributed microcopy or an arbitrary image percentage. N already requires reallocation before shrinking copy. |
| Title-only input | Use the supplied title's meaning/state/relationship to form an image or visual structure unless the user excludes it. Do not invent dates, places, participants, slogans or an event narrative. Title-only input is not the same as a requested typography-only output. |
| Explicit image-free / typography-only | Honor the exclusion of imagery; do not insert an icon or texture as a compliance token. Judge typography and space within that request. This removes the nonglyph requirement, while the default rejected template remains excluded unless the current explicit instruction actually requires that whole system. |
| Portrait format | Design the reading/crop relationship for the requested ratio and available copy. The portrait source JPEGs are not permission to stretch/crop them into landscape or to force vertical titles. N already separates ratio-specific design from enlargement of one common template. |
| Explicitly requested style | Preserve the actual requested style, including strong colour, 3D, clay, serif or gothic when requested. A font or “modern/clean” label does not alone require the rejected composition. If the current instruction explicitly specifies the rejected whole relationship, use the existing scoped exception; do not demand a special waiver phrase or new approval. Do not silently replace the request with N's research preferences. |

None of these boundary cases was newly generated here. These are constraints on reusable guidance, not claimed successful transfer results.
