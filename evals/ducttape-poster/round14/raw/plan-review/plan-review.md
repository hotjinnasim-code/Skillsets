## Human whole-template exclusion

The human explicitly rejects a passive solid/near-solid field dominated by large gothic title, date and information blocks, with photographs attached as subordinate small cells or a bottom strip. Split colour, faint texture, gradients, grids, or a title/all-font replacement do not resolve unchanged organisation. Both Canva compositions are rejected whole-template counterexamples, including the serif Street Portraits layout. There is no override in the pinned request.

The relevant positive question is: **what visible relation between scene or image editing, viewpoint, crop, space, type and information determines the whole composition?** A large picture, picture presence, a serif title, or words such as “cinematic,” “optical” and “panorama” cannot answer that question. Conversely, readable information groups, plain negative space and gothic glyphs are not violations in themselves.

The plan's image-removal counterfactuals are useful prompts, but insufficient evidence. Most image-based designs lose meaning when their image is removed. Their remaining information may also remain perfectly usable, which is desirable. The distinction is whether the actual scene/editing/structure determines boundaries, visual weight and placement, rather than serving as a replaceable illustration behind or beside a pre-existing title/information panel.

No universal grid, minimum font size, title-first route, pointing guide, transformation sequence, or extra five-style roster is imposed here. Internal coordinates, font choices, line counts and scene-feature counts are feasibility suggestions, not additional client constraints.
