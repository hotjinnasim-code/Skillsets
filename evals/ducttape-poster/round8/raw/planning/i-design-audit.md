## Decision

The rewrite is substantially reusable and worth retaining. It addresses every requested visual dimension, treats references as complete systems, distinguishes research from client assets, preserves literal copy, and explicitly lowers both the clay/complement/toy package and the repeated large-object/large-title/footer package without banning those media. It does not embed the repair-event's five media as a permanent roster. The concurrency, single-image output, finite correction budget, complete-set inspection, and one-request/five-image evaluation scope are also appropriate.

The proposed five image structures are genuinely plausible alternatives at the planning level: continuous observational scene, crossing flat actions, a joined textile surface, a distributed catalogue, and a sequential comic. They are more than material swaps. However, their **type hierarchy and overall temperament remain too similar**: small or medium quiet titles, regular front-facing information, restrained warm color in most roles, and numerous adjacency-based information groups. That creates a second quiet-editorial default. The current plan should receive a small focused revision before generation; it does not need a new fixed five-style list or a twenty-image campaign.

## High-priority fixes before generation

### 1. Give M2 and M4 stronger, different hierarchy roles

Keep M1, M3, and M5 as the quieter counterpoints. Strengthen the following two directions while retaining their current, different whole-page structures. This is a correction for **this brief**, not a reusable rule that those two media must always have these hierarchies.

**M2: make event identification and practical information dominate the colored exchange composition.** Keep the crossing hands/objects and diagonal movement. Replace the mostly-small-title/nearby-information treatment with one emphatic, intact **동네 수선·교환 모임** group in a large high-contrast color region; the date/time/location group is the next unmistakable read. The poetic title can remain a smaller horizontal companion. Thus the first read answers what the event is, followed by when/where, while the crossing action makes exchange visible. Do not repeat isolated numerals or extract new slogans from the supplied copy. Keep the information upright and usable even though the image moves diagonally.

For M2's local palette, a confident single saturated field, such as vivid cobalt, with warm white and near-black provides a stronger information/color voice without automatically returning to the teal/red complementary pairing already suggested in the plan. Choose contrast and object separation deliberately; this color suggestion is not a skill default. The hands/garment/book need clear silhouettes against that field. Avoid turning the information into small stickers floating around a large central cartoon.

**M4: make the supplied title a major typographic anchor of the distributed catalogue.** Keep several cut-out objects and unequal image/information modules. Use the complete **한 번 더 쓰는 하루** as the largest, confident horizontal type anchor along the lower edge of the modular field, spanning more than one module's width. Retain readable event-description and free-participation groups as conspicuous companions. Put the schedule/location and bring/activity groups inside the distributed module system. This creates an image-index/type relationship with an emphatic anchor, rather than M3's modest long serif line or M5's title beside three narrative frames.

The M4 anchor must not produce a single hero object above a tiny footer. Keep the object field distributed, the module dimensions unequal, and the useful copy substantial. A firm dark type/bright ground contrast can provide the dominance; artificial ageing or an added accent color is not required. The supplied event descriptor and **참가비 없음** should make this read as a free community gathering rather than a retail catalogue. Do not add prices, codes, labels, or marketing copy.

These revisions give M2 an event-information-led, saturated diagonal composition and M4 a title-led, distributed editorial composition. They restore purposeful display strength without making all five loud or recreating the same hero/title formula.

## Reusability adjustments worth making in the skill

1. **Make the positive hierarchy choice explicit.** In the short direction specification, ask for the first read, the next useful read, the dominant type role, and the image/type boundary that gives the layout its identity. The existing fields mention relative weight, but the current plan shows how all five can still converge on medium/small neutral titles. Do not add a fixed minimum type size, permanent two-loud/three-quiet quota, or mandatory title-led variant.

2. **Clarify what lowering a default means.** Keep both named packages lowered, and explicitly allow large display type, strong color, cartoon imagery, photographic shadow, or rough material when the content and whole composition justify them. The skill already mostly makes this distinction; a brief positive sentence near the set comparison would prevent the current overcorrection into quiet neutral editorial work. Do not replace the old package with a new cream/ink/serif/material-macro roster.

3. **Soften the substitution diagnostic into a question about fit.** “If another event name can replace this one, mostly unchanged” is useful evidence of arbitrary decoration, but legitimate grid, photographic, and identity systems can transfer across related events. Judge whether the chosen structure, visual relation, and information priorities answer this brief; do not demand a unique literal metaphor or a visible human action for every subject. Retain the substantive nonglyph requirement and the prohibition on using tiny decoration to evade it.

4. **Keep references as decisions, not reusable style labels.** The source table's explicit limits are good. For the current plan, the identical Canva sentence repeated under every role is only a broad audience principle, not five distinct adapted observations. The coordinator can retain its already-viewed Canva template while stating, per role, how people identify the event, find the calendar/location, and understand what to bring. No need to attach Canva art or import its stock photo/title layout into all roles.

5. **Compare all five as one set after the two revisions.** Retain the closest-role comparison, but briefly check all pairings for repeated focal size, title position/voice, information path, and dominant image shape. M4/M5 must not collapse into the same horizontal row cadence; M1/M3 must not collapse into a material image beside a pale copy panel. This is a content-sensitive comparison, not a new universal five-layout taxonomy.

## Successful parts to retain

- Whole-system studio research includes image style, crop, color, layout, information, typography, material, and emotion; Canva is an audience/communication reference rather than a finish certificate.
- The research/client-asset distinction is explicit. Author names, source copy, logos, and exact layouts do not become generation instructions or preservation gates.
- Five complete independent posters, actual maker-role execution within available concurrency, exact requested quantity/ratio, no contact-sheet substitution, and bounded corrections are clear.
- Literal copy and readable Korean remain hard requirements. Large-form or type experimentation cannot justify deleting, duplicating, fragmenting, or inventing the supplied information.
- The design criteria recognize intentional roughness and expressive media; neutral paper/serif/minimalism do not earn automatic high-end status.
- The one-request/five-image evaluation loop avoids prematurely scaling this into twenty images, distinguishes ordinary making from external evaluation, and does not claim success from a few good members.

After the focused hierarchy, source-observation, copy-role, and status fixes, proceed with the authorized single five-image request. Judge the actual images and the weakest member; this audit cannot establish that the proposed styles, Korean typography, hands, repair details, or public-event readability will survive generation.
