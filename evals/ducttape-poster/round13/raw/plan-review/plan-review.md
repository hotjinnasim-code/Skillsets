## Architecture/light and exhibition-plus-talk coherence

- **M1:** The shared room, original architecture photographs, small listening group and window/floor light give the two activities a direct common setting. The scene is substantial, not a tiny icon. It should read as an approachable event rather than a stock property photograph. A hand action is unnecessary.
- **M2:** The audience's viewpoint makes light and depth observable. Distant photographs and seats can support the exhibition/talk identity without staging people. Their visibility is uncertain at the proposed full-screen scale, and empty seats alone do not prove a talk. The combined-event copy and shared-room cues may establish the relationship; the plan does not require a literal audience scene. A generic luxury interior would lose the community-event connection.
- **M3:** A far view and near detail can make the pleasure of architectural observation concrete. Both must plausibly refer to the same opening/light boundary; two unrelated nice photographs would weaken the comparison. This is correspondence between viewpoints, not a before/after process or causal transformation. The combined-event copy can connect observation with explanation; additional people or a third activity image are not compulsory.
- **M4:** The central room directly explains shared exhibition and listening use alongside the window/light relationship. Cutting away a wall is a drawing convention, not an event in which the building physically changes. Readable opening depth, exhibition surface and listening area matter more than matching a planned number of seats or windows. Dense professional drawing symbols would conflict with the approachable adult purpose and the no-added-copy condition.
- **M5:** Opening depth, wall thickness and a connected floor-light boundary give the lower band substantive architectural content. The exhibition and explanation concern that observation subject, supported by the combined-event copy above. A literal photograph inside this graphic is not required. Detached stripes, decorative glyphs or arbitrary wedges would fail to carry the planned subject. Photographic realism or a measured ray simulation is not an added requirement.

All five have a plausible content-bearing nonglyph construction. Their success depends on visible relationships in the eventual poster, not the scene description alone. Neither strong colour nor restraint is itself evidence of audience fit.
